# Warhammer Generative Geometry: Research Handoff

## Purpose

This package preserves a developing research program: model Warhammer 40,000 as a high-dimensional, rule-governed geometric system whose visible board outcomes arise through successive projections.

The package supports four uses:

1. Resume the conversation with an LLM or human collaborator.
2. Separate established mathematics from proposed terminology and speculative claims.
3. Build a computational prototype from a small rules subset.
4. Design falsifiable tests before expanding into full army, mission, terrain, and policy models.

## Core thesis

A Warhammer game can be represented as a context-indexed family of legal state trajectories. Unit characteristics, weapon characteristics, army rules, mission rules, terrain, deployment, actions, and dice jointly define a structured state-transition system. Victory points form a low-dimensional projection of this system.

Raw tournament win rates collapse many variables into one aggregate. A geometric and operator-based model preserves structure before aggregation. The proposed method seeks predictive compression, causal diagnosis, and decision support from sparse rule data plus explicit constraints.

## Most important mathematical correction

The line construction discussed during the conversation, in which lines connect matched tick marks on perpendicular axes, creates an envelope of a one-parameter family of lines. In the equal-axis case, the envelope is parabola-like after coordinate transformation. It is not generally a circle. Rotation does not automatically produce a sphere.

The correction strengthens the project. The useful object is the envelope generated by a family of constraints. The exact curve depends on the line-pairing rule, axis scaling, parameterization, and metric.

## Immediate recommended starting point

Build a minimal exact model with one attacker, one weapon, one target, no spatial movement, and no special rules. Represent attack count, hit threshold, Strength, Toughness, save, armour penetration, damage, wounds, and D6 outcomes. Then add one coupling rule at a time.

The first prototype should answer five questions:

1. Can the rule sequence compile into a composable operator graph?
2. Can exact outcome distributions be reproduced from first principles?
3. Does the proposed pair-strain coordinate simplify Strength-Toughness relations?
4. Which rules preserve factorization, and which rules create cross-stage coupling?
5. Can a counterfactual diagnostic identify the smallest change needed for a materially different board effect?

## Boundary of present claims

No claim in this package proves a novel mathematical theory. The package defines a research language and a testable computational program. Several ideas may reduce to known methods from stochastic games, control theory, formal methods, probabilistic graphical models, discrete geometry, or operations research. Novelty may emerge from synthesis, representation, or application rather than from new mathematics.
